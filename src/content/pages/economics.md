## Master thesis

**Does an Agent-Based Model Provide Incremental Predictive Information in Macroeconomic Forecasting?**
Master of Science in Applied Economics, University of Neuchâtel, January 2026. Supervised by Prof. Daniel Kaufmann.

[Download the thesis (PDF, 51 pages)](/files/josh-levent-msc-thesis-2026.pdf)

<details class="abstract">
<summary>Abstract</summary>

This thesis examines whether a large-scale agent-based model (ABM) forecast contains predictive information that is not already embedded in standard macroeconomic forecasting benchmarks. Using the publicly available Austrian forecasts of Poledna et al. (2023) for quarterly GDP growth and inflation, I compare the ABM signal to DSGE and reduced-form benchmarks (AR and a newly estimated BVAR), as well as a naive no-change forecast, across horizons up to 12 quarters. Beyond accuracy comparisons, I implement optimality diagnostics and forecast-encompassing regressions (pairwise and joint), using HAC inference for multi-step horizons and Holm–Bonferroni adjustments for multiple testing. The evidence suggests that the ABM forecast for inflation is not redundant and contributes incremental predictive content relative to competing benchmarks. The evidence for GDP growth forecasts does not meet conventional levels of statistical significance. The results are limited by weak statistical power but suggest that the ABM forecasts contain some incremental information at least some of the time. This complements previous findings on accuracy and highlights the potential value of ABMs as a forecasting tool. However, in addition to finding only weak evidence, the generalisability of these results is limited by the use of a single ABM, a single country, and a forecast window that contains an unusual macro environment.

</details>

## Research interests

My longer-term interest is in simulations of human economic behaviour that can guide policy making: whether computer simulations of whole societies can tell us something about how our world compares with hypothetical alternatives, so that we have a better way of judging new political ideas.

Related to this, I am interested in whole-economy digital twins that track real-world entities and their attributes, as a way to improve government and central bank statistics and models. The Macroeconomic Outlook above is a first step in that direction: before you can model the economy you need its data in a usable form.

Some of the simulation programs I have written in this area are published [on HASH](https://hash.ai/@joshlevent) and [on AnyLogic Cloud](https://cloud.anylogic.com/models?public=true&customSearchType=USER&customSearchData=bfc0fd2f-4c42-43fd-9d45-5e584beb0bfd). In 2023 I attended the Sant'Anna School of Advanced Studies summer school on agent-based models in economics in Pisa.

I'm glad to hear from social and computer scientists working on social behaviour, machine learning, macroeconomics or agent-based modelling.
